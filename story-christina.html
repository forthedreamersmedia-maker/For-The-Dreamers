The breakdown she thought was a mistake ended up changing her family's mind.

Christina· Fourth-year psychology major, pre-med track· UC Riverside· Riverside, CA

Christina came into college wanting to be a therapist. Somewhere between organic chemistry and a breakdown she was sure would ruin everything, she ended up on a different path — one she's still not entirely certain about, and has made peace with that.

Watch the full interview on YouTube · ~20 min

What got you interested in psychology?

Children's mental health, and mental health in general — how we act, why we act that way. That's what pulled me in.

Did you feel pressure to have your career figured out early?

Some came from my family — I'm from a traditional Asian household, where there's an expectation to know your path early. But honestly, I put more pressure on myself than anyone else did.

What surprised you about psychology as a major?

The math. I assumed it would be pure humanities, but between general chemistry and the STEM requirements for pre-med, there was a lot more logic and rigor than I expected.

Did you ever doubt your path?

Junior year, organic chemistry nearly convinced me I'd picked the wrong one. I thought about business, nursing, therapy instead of psychiatry. I've realized those doubts are just part of being 18 to 22 — you don't actually know who you want to be yet, no matter how sure you sound.

What mistake ended up helping you the most?

I had a full breakdown in front of my family. At the time I thought it was the worst thing I could've done — my family doesn't always know how to handle emotions like that. But it's what got me into therapy. Saying how I actually felt changed how open they were about mental health after that.

What would you tell a high schooler interested in psychology who doesn't know where to start?

Watch videos, talk to people already in the field, look into the different areas within it. I still don't know exactly which direction I'm headed — just follow whatever keeps pulling your interest.

One piece of advice to your past self?

Slow down and actually reflect. My first two years of college I only looked forward — next thing, next thing — and never looked back at how far I'd come.
